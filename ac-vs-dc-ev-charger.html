AC vs DC EV Charger: Complete Comparison Guide

Compare charging speed, cost, connectors and the best use cases for home and commercial EV charging.

Quick answer: An AC EV charger delivers alternating current to the vehicle's onboard charger, taking 3–8 hours for a full charge. A DC fast charger converts power externally and delivers direct current to the battery, reaching 80% charge in 30–90 minutes. AC suits home and workplace use; DC suits commercial and fleet applications.

Most people buying an EV charger for the first time assume faster is always better. It is a reasonable starting point, but it can lead to over-specified home installations and under-specified commercial setups. The right charger depends on how long the vehicle is parked, what the electrical supply can support, and what needs to happen during a charging session.

This guide explains the real differences between AC and DC EV chargers—how each works, their infrastructure cost, where each belongs, and how to make the decision without overcomplicating it.

What Is an AC EV Charger?

An AC EV charger supplies alternating current from the grid to the vehicle through a Type 2 connector. The vehicle's onboard charger converts that AC to DC before storing it in the battery. This conversion step limits charging speed: most India-market EVs handle between 3.3 kW and 7.2 kW, while a smaller number support up to 22 kW.

Common power outputs

  • 3.3 kW: standard for basic home units and two-wheelers.
  • 7.2 kW: the most widely used residential and workplace option.
  • 11 kW: suited for commercial properties with three-phase supply.
  • 22 kW: for vehicles with higher onboard charger capacity, such as Hyundai Ioniq 5 and Kia EV6.

Best applications: homes, apartments, offices, hotels and any location where vehicles park for several hours. The vehicle charges gradually while the driver is sleeping, working or dining.

Advantages

  • Lower equipment cost.
  • Simpler electrical infrastructure; most homes support a 7.2 kW charger on single-phase supply.
  • A gentler charging rate that supports long-term battery health.
  • Type 2 compatibility with every four-wheel EV sold in India.

Limitations

  • A 4–8 hour charge time means it suits extended parking only.
  • It is not suitable for short dwell times or high session-throughput locations.

What Is a DC Fast Charger?

A DC fast charger handles AC-to-DC conversion inside the charger and delivers direct current straight to the battery, bypassing the onboard charger. Since the conversion happens in the charger rather than the car, significantly more power can be transferred and charging times drop dramatically.

The dominant DC connector in India is CCS2 (Combined Charging System 2), used by Tata, MG, Hyundai, BYD, Mahindra, Kia, BMW and most other EVs sold in India from 2021 onwards. Some older or imported models use CHAdeMO, while select BYD variants use GB/T.

Common power outputs

  • 30 kW: entry-level commercial charging; a Tata Nexon EV reaches 80% in about 75–90 minutes.
  • 50–60 kW: the most common commercial specification across India.
  • 120 kW+: for highway corridors, large fleet depots and high-volume charging hubs.

Best applications: fuel stations, malls, quick-turnaround hotels, fleet depots and highway charging points—anywhere the available charging window is 30–60 minutes rather than several hours.

Advantages

  • 80% charge in 30–90 minutes, depending on vehicle and output.
  • Higher session throughput per bay, serving more vehicles per day.
  • Essential for fleets that need vehicles back in service quickly.

Limitations

  • Higher equipment cost and demanding electrical infrastructure, often including a dedicated supply or transformer.
  • Not required or cost-effective for overnight home charging.
  • Faster charge rates generate more battery heat, so repeated use is not ideal as the only charging method.

Which EV Charger Is Best for Home?

For most Indian homes, a 7.2 kW AC charger is the right answer. Tata Nexon EV, MG ZS EV, Mahindra XUV400 and most EVs sold in India accept up to 7.2 kW AC through their onboard charger. A faster AC charger will not charge them more quickly because the bottleneck is inside the vehicle.

A DC fast charger at home is unnecessary and expensive. A 50 kW unit requires significantly more equipment and electrical infrastructure for a car that is parked overnight anyway. A 7.2 kW charger on a dedicated 32A circuit gives most Indian EV owners a full battery by morning; 3.3 kW is sufficient for short daily commutes. Consider 11 kW or 22 kW AC only if your vehicle supports higher AC acceptance and the property has three-phase supply.

Which EV Charger Is Best for Businesses?

The answer depends on how long vehicles stay at your location.

  • Hotels: use AC chargers for overnight guests; choose DC at quick-turnaround drive-through locations.
  • Fleet operators: DC fast charging is necessary when vehicles must return to service in under 90 minutes.
  • Fuel stations: a DC Fast Charger is the standard for walk-in EV customers.
  • Shopping malls: DC fits a 45–90 minute visit, while AC works in staff parking.
  • Corporate offices: 7.2 kW AC chargers suit employees parked for a full workday.
  • Commercial buildings: mixed-use properties can benefit from AC for long-stay users and DC for short-stay visitors.

How to Choose the Right EV Charger

1. Check your vehicle

Review the EV's AC and DC acceptance rates. A Tata Nexon EV does not benefit from above 7.2 kW AC or 50 kW DC, while a Hyundai Ioniq 5 benefits from 11 kW AC and 100+ kW DC.

2. Consider parking time

For overnight or 8+ hour parking, AC is sufficient and more cost-effective. For under two hours, DC is the only option that delivers a useful charge.

3. Review available electrical supply

Single-phase supply supports up to 7.2 kW AC. Three-phase supply is required for 11 kW AC and above. DC fast chargers need dedicated supply and significant load headroom: a 50 kW charger needs around 60–70 kW of sanctioned load.

4. Set the equipment and infrastructure budget

AC equipment and infrastructure costs are substantially lower. For homes and offices, AC is usually the right financial decision; for revenue-generating commercial locations, DC's throughput can justify the investment.

5. Plan for future expansion

Size a home circuit for a possible second EV. Commercial operators should design shared switchgear and earthing for the final number of charging bays, not just the first charger.

Final Recommendation

Choose AC if:

  • You charge at home overnight.
  • The vehicle is parked for 4+ hours at an office, hotel or society.
  • Your supply is single-phase.
  • Budget is important and charging speed is not urgent.

Choose DC if:

  • Your location serves drivers who need a quick charge and leave.
  • You run a fleet requiring under-90-minute turnaround.
  • You operate a revenue-generating public charging station.
  • The location is on a highway or arterial route.

The common mistake is over-specifying a DC fast charger where AC would be sufficient and cheaper, or under-specifying slow AC chargers at a public location where drivers need speed. Match the charger to how the vehicle is actually used at that location.
